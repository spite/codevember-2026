"Cocoon"

A figure keeps dancing while silk winds round it, feet first, in two sets of crossing bands that widen until they close over the head. As the wall thickens the silk holds the body back, until a finished cocoon all but stops it. A small lamp circles it. In front of the figure the lamp lights the felt; behind it, the silk glows amber where it is thin and dull red where the body is thick, and the limbs show through as shadows. A finished cocoon holds for a few seconds and hatches: the figure strains against the shell, which splits open at the breastbone and breaks into flakes that spiral down round it, bouncing off the limbs. The figure comes out in a new colour and a new dance, and is wound again.

Controls: drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, `R` hatches a new one, `M` toggles the sound, `Space` pauses. The panel sets the look, the motion (a walk, a run, a sneak and eight dances), the silk's thickness, the floss, the lamp and its orbit, and the animation speed. With auto change on, a closed cocoon hatches "Change every" seconds (4 by default) after it closes, counted again from any interaction, so a new one comes about every 21 s; with it off, a finished cocoon stays. Left alone for a few seconds, the camera comes round to the dancer's front and sways there.

Notable:

- The figure is three.js's Xbot example model (a Mixamo character). Its walk, run and two additive poses are its own; the eight dances (the samba from three's Michelle example) are retargeted to its skeleton and stored as raw floats (`assets/*.json` + `*.bin`).
- `PipelineMaterial` knows nothing of skinning, so each material's `displace()` skins with three's own bone texture. In the prepass, a copy of last frame's bone matrices gives the skinned motion a velocity.
- The silk is the body's own mesh drawn a second time, skinned by the same bones and pushed out along normals smoothed across the seams. Where it is on is a pattern in the rest pose (turn counts from height, angle and distance from the axis), so the bands stay put on the moving body and also cross outstretched arms.
- The felt is a dome over each of a scatter of points at two scales. Its slope is known, so it takes one lookup per scale; it is turned to the world by the bones' rotation, so the relief moves with the body.
- The lamp is not one of the pipeline's lights. Each frame the figure is drawn twice from it, as depth: once with everything that casts a shadow, once with the body alone. The difference between where the light went in and where a point is gives how much of the figure it crossed, and whether that included the body or only silk. Blue is lost first, so thin parts glow amber and thick ones a dull red.
- The floss is geometry: thousands of three-sided tubes rooted on the skin, carrying their root's bone weights. Their free ends trail on springs, one per bone, and swing back. They are blended and kept out of the prepass and the shadow maps. Fewer are built on medium and low quality.
- Webs form where two wound parts of the body come close, stretch and neck as they part, and tear. Fast parts throw off wisps.
- Hatching, every patch of the shell (a Voronoi cell in the rest pose) has its moment, sooner nearer the front, the chest and the trunk. Each patch sinks onto the skin just before it goes, so a hole never shows a hollow shell. As each goes, chips of it are let go on spirals round the figure.
- The body is also 16 capsules between bones. Chips that meet a limb bounce off it and carry its motion; the lamp steps round the body instead of passing through a hand. The camera is kept inside the cyc.
- The figure changes colour every time it is in a cocoon: one of eight hues, at least 90° round from the last. A walk, run or sneak is always followed by a dance.
- Sound: cloth rustles as the wound body moves and as the silk goes on (louder the faster and the thicker), a muffled heartbeat for each shove from inside, cloth ripping as the shell breaks, and paper fluttering and falling with the chips.
