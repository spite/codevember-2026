"Erosion"

Suspended beach glass: wave-tumbled pebbles, curved bottle shards, fluted pressed-glass remnants, swirled marbles, bottle stoppers, bottle-neck rings, tori and latticino cane fragments (white spiral threads inside) floating weightlessly through each other. Every piece is rendered in a multi-layer screen-space composite pass so pieces behind other pieces are refracted, dispersed, Beer-Lambert absorbed and heavily diffused through the frosted, micro-pitted glass in front of them.

Controls: drag a piece to push it through the others (it is held by the point you grabbed, so it swings round and keeps its spin when let go), click a piece to nudge and spin it, drag empty space to orbit, scroll to zoom, W for the wireframe, M for sound, R for a new mix; while paused, a drag anywhere orbits. The panel adjusts piece count, colour palette, backdrop, Erosion (morphing freshly fractured angular shards into wave-rounded pebbles in real time), Pattern wear, and ranges for Frosting, Wetness (dry powdery silica rind vs. wet satin sheen), Diffusion, Refraction, Dispersion, Translucency, Drift and Camera speed.

Notable:

- Dual-pose procedural erosion geometry (`glass.js`): each piece computes both its freshly fractured/angular pose (`aRawPos`) and its wave-tumbled eroded pose (`position`), along with local optical thickness (`aThickness`) and surface exposure/wear (`aWear`), allowing the Erosion slider to morph every piece smoothly in the vertex shader even while paused. The eroded pose is Taubin-smoothed over the mesh, so pressed-glass relief and fracture steps wear down to soft undulations instead of creases. Each pose is also kept without its pattern (pressed-glass relief, threads, ribs, stopper facets, marble veins), so the shader erodes the shape with Erosion and the pattern at its own rate: Pattern wear 0.5 erodes them together, higher wears the pattern away first, lower keeps it on a worn body. Pieces start from a subdivided sphere of detail 20 on high (14 medium, 12 low; thin shells, rods, stoppers and marbles get 1.4–1.5x), and a new set is generated a few ms per frame, then swapped in, so a change never stalls the page.
- Greedy screen-space layer partitioning (`render.js`): pieces are sorted back-to-front and grouped into non-overlapping screen-space layers. Where the view runs along the glass (edge-on pieces, the ends of a ring or torus), the transmitted light is lifted to a saturated glow, as light piped inside the glass would be, instead of going dark. Rings and tori are ray traced inside: the shader fits an ideal torus to the piece (wobble and thread included), refracts the view ray into it, marches it through the tube, out through the far wall or back in by total internal reflection, into the other side and out again, then reads what is behind along the final direction. The fitted cross-section is a superellipse, so it follows the collars' squarer corners. Rays enter at the fitted surface's smooth normal, since the path is too sensitive for the frost's pits; frosted pieces average two rays spread over a cone, and each marches to the first inner wall once, then follows both the transmitted and the Fresnel-reflected ray from there, so the image stays continuous across the critical angle. Frost scatters what the tracer resolves: the traced view blends back towards the diffuse look of the other pieces by the frost amount (up to 85%), so frosted tori show only a hint of the lensing inside and wet ones stay glassy. The tracer lives in its own shader variant (`TRACE`), since compiled into the shared glass shader it made every piece about three times slower, and every program is compiled behind the loading screen. Depth from earlier layers is kept, so a piece that is partly behind another stays hidden there even when their centres swap order. Each layer builds a 6-level Gaussian blur pyramid (down to 1/32 resolution with bicubic upsampling) of everything behind it, computes exact additive world-space ray thickness, and shades each piece with micro-pitted Voronoi percussion normals, chromatic dispersion, Beer-Lambert absorption and forward-scattered internal light. After the first layer, the pyramid, the layer copy and the thickness clear are limited (scissored) to the screen rect the previous layer drew over, the only part that changed. While nothing the composite reads changes (paused, view still), it reuses its last image: 0.13 ms a frame instead of about 3.4 on high. The camera follows the cluster: it eases towards the cluster's centre and to the distance that fits its radius (90th-percentile piece reach) in the narrower angle of view, so every aspect frames the whole set; the drift and the visitor's own orbit and zoom stay on top.
- Suspended-in-water physics (`physics.js`): each piece is dragged toward a slow, divergence-free current (small pieces follow it faster) and turned toward its vorticity, exaggerated for the tumbling of lopsided pieces; a soft push between bounding spheres keeps them spaced like water would, and a spring holds the cluster in view. Contacts come from each piece's own shape (`collide.js`): a coarse signed distance grid of the worn and of the broken pose, blended by erosion like the mesh, and 128 points on its surface; two pieces touch where one's points reach into the other's field, so bowls and rings collide as they look. The push-out follows the deepest reach along the depth-weighted normal, and impulses act at the contact point through each piece's mass and rotational inertia, with Coulomb friction. On load the fields build over the first frames, while the pieces are still apart.
- Each piece has its own look: the glass sliders are ranges, and every piece sits at its own seeded place inside each one, with its own erosion and pattern wear. A new mix comes with a wave: as it rushes in the whole cluster is swept off-screen across the view, staggered from the leading edge; as it draws back the backwash carries the new set in from the same side, every piece in its freshly broken shape; they rest a beat (1.5 s) after landing, then each erodes over 9 s, to a bed of grinding and water (loops cut from the knife-grind strokes and the surf's backwash) that swells in as the wearing starts, rises a little in pitch as it gets finer and fades as the pieces come out smooth. The new set is generated while the old one leaves, collision shapes included, and settled on its own out of sight (3 s of physics), so its pieces fly in, farthest places first and passing through one another, to positions the physics won't push them out of; overlaps that do arise ease apart (at most 0.004 per step) instead of snapping. Changing the Pieces slider adds or removes pieces one at a time instead. Sliders moved by hand apply to every piece.
- Loops in the glass shaders take their bounds from uniforms, since the D3D compiler otherwise unrolls them (the shaders took 5-6 s to compile).
- Sound: every contact between pieces is heard, by how long it lasts: a short one (under 0.15 s) is a pebble click when it ends (a tick when light), as loud as its hardest impact and a little higher the smaller the pieces; a longer one grinds, a short grind first and then, while the pieces stay in contact and keep sliding, a grinding stroke now and then. Clicks and grinds each have a budget (clicks 1.6 a second, bursts of 2), and at most two clicks ring at once, at least 90 ms apart, so the cluster never chatters. Panned by where it happens and quieter farther away. Contacts come from the pieces' real shapes, so a sound means the surfaces really meet. Sources on freesound.org: clicks and ticks from "SFX_stone_pebble_gravel.wav" by EricsSoundschmiede (669451, CC0), low-passed to a duller knock (brightness centred around 2.3 kHz instead of 4.5), short grinds from "Rusty Grind Sound" and "Short Grind Sound 2" by qubodup (743250, 743256, CC0), and grinding strokes from "knife being sharpened on rough grinding stone" by 2100385_Joshua_Blignaut (593016, CC BY-NC 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/: credited here, non-commercial use only).
- Soundscape: under the glass, a loop of surf on a pebble beach ("surf and pebbles on beach.wav" by odilonmarcenaro, freesound.org 121102, CC0; the wave-in and its backwash are overlapping cuts of one stretch of the same recording, at one level, so the second picks up as the first fades), cut to 60 s with its end crossfaded into its start; the Shore slider sets its level, and it follows the sound button.
- Environments: besides the two studios, four beach HDRIs from Poly Haven (CC0, 1k): Spiaggia di Mondello, Secluded Beach, Fish Hoek Beach and Umhlanga Sunrise. Umhlanga Sunrise is the default, behind the glass blurred (BG blur 0.25). Each loads when first chosen (all four are fetched in the background once the page is up) and keeps its own intensity, with the key light tinted by its sky. R picks one of the beaches (and keeps the backdrop); a change crossfades over 2.5 s, blending the old and new maps in the backdrop and reflections and easing the ambient and key light. Environment radiance is clamped where the glass reads it, since an HDRI's sun can exceed what half-float buffers hold.
- Camera: it starts 9 units out on the far side of the glass from the sun (each beach's sun is located at its brightest pixel, which also places the key light), so the sun backlights the cluster. It then drifts calmly around that view, a slow sway, rise and fall and breathing in distance from incommensurate sines, applied as per-frame changes so it adds to the visitor's own orbiting; Camera speed sets its pace.
- Pieces (a collapsed panel section): the count and a weight for each kind (pebbles, bottle and bowl shards, shards, pressed glass, marbles, stoppers, bottle necks, tori, canes). New sets draw their kinds by these weights; a second after they stop changing, a wave brings a set with the same seed and the new mix. R rerolls them: the common kinds (pebbles, bottle, plain and bowl shards, pressed glass) from 0.3 to 10, leaning low so one or two dominate; the oddities up to 0.6, except that in about a third of the sets one of them is the find, at 2 to 6.
